Is a remote build cache worth it?

On the small projects we measured, a filled cache took about a quarter off a Gradle build, and an empty one cost about a second. Here are the numbers and the runs that produced them. They are from our test projects on a CI machine, so measure your own.

Gradle: what a cache saved and what a miss cost

We ran the same Gradle build three ways, seven times each, and timed the whole command.

SetupFastestMedianSlowest
No remote cache17.06 s17.54 s18.17 s
Remote cache, empty (a miss)16.83 s18.54 s18.67 s
Remote cache, filled (a hit)12.79 s13.41 s13.60 s

By the medians, a miss cost about one second (about 6%) and a hit saved about four (about 24%). The miss build was slower in 6 of 7 rounds. The run and its log are public.

What we ran: a GitHub-hosted CI machine, 4 vCPUs, 16 GB; Gradle 9.8.0, Java 21; FosterStack Cache 0.2.1 on the same machine. The project had 4 modules of 150 generated classes each, and only the compile tasks ran. It compiles quickly and every build also paid Gradle's start-up, so a bigger project may show different percentages. Details: does a remote cache slow your build when it misses?

What this tells you, and what it does not

  • A cache that is empty or down makes builds slower, not wrong. See what happens when the server is down.
  • Results are from small test projects on one kind of CI machine. We did not test large projects or other CI products.
  • Your saving depends on how much of your build is work that can be skipped. Turn it on in CI first, then read your own hit rate: how to measure it.
